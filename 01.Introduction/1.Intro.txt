============================================================
  INTRODUCTION TO MONTE CARLO SIMULATION
============================================================

To predict a stock price, every possible outcome must be considered.
To simulate how a particle moves after a collision, every interaction
matters. The space of outcomes is vast and continuous — you simply
cannot enumerate all of it.

This is where Monte Carlo Simulation offers an elegant idea:

  "Don't enumerate everything. Instead, randomly sample possibilities,
   compute the outcome for each sample, and average the results.
   The Law of Large Numbers guarantees convergence."

At 10 samples       → the result is noisy
At 1,000 samples    → the result is close
At 1,000,000 samples → the result is essentially exact

------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT MONTE CARLO ACTUALLY COMPUTES
------------------------------------------------------------

Monte Carlo computes an EXPECTED VALUE — the average outcome
over a large number of random trials.

------------------------------------------------------------
WHY TRADITIONAL INTEGRATION FAILS
------------------------------------------------------------

Traditional grid-based integration breaks down when:
  1. The space is too large (too many dimensions)
  2. Probabilities are unknown or hard to define
  3. The function itself is too complex to integrate analytically

The core problem is the "Curse of Dimensionality":
  Grid-based methods require points that grow EXPONENTIALLY
  with the number of dimensions.

  2 dimensions,  10 points each → 100 evaluations
  10 dimensions, 10 points each → 10,000,000,000 evaluations
  100 dimensions, 10 points each → more evaluations than atoms in the universe

Monte Carlo breaks this curse entirely.
The number of samples needed depends only on the desired accuracy —
NOT on the number of dimensions.
Whether you're working in 10 or 100 dimensions, the same number of
samples gives you the same quality of answer.

------------------------------------------------------------
THE CLASSIC EXAMPLE — ESTIMATING π
------------------------------------------------------------

Imagine a square of side length 2 centered at the origin.
Inside it sits a circle of radius 1.

  Area of circle   = π × 1² = π
  Area of square   = 2 × 2  = 4

  → Area of circle / Area of square = π / 4

Now throw darts randomly at the square.
Count how many land inside the circle.

  π ≈ 4 × (darts inside circle / total darts)

The more darts you throw, the closer you get to the real π.
This will be implemented step by step later in the project.

------------------------------------------------------------
KEY FORMULA TO REMEMBER
------------------------------------------------------------

  Error in the estimate = 1 / sqrt(total_samples)

  This means:
    - 100 samples   → error ≈ 0.1    (10%)
    - 10,000 samples → error ≈ 0.01   (1%)
    - 1,000,000 samples → error ≈ 0.001  (0.1%)

  To cut the error in half, you need 4× as many samples.
  Accuracy improves slowly — but it always improves.
