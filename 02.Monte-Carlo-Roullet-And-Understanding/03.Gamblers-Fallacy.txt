============================================================
  GAMBLER'S FALLACY
============================================================

THE FAMOUS INCIDENT:

  "On August 18, 1913, at the casino in Monte Carlo, black came up
  a record twenty-six times in succession [in roulette]... There was
  a near-panicky rush to bet on red, beginning around the time black
  had come up a phenomenal fifteen times in a row."
                                    — Huff and Geis, How to Take a Chance

Everyone in that casino made the same mistake.
They believed red was now "due" — that the universe owed them
a correction. It does not.

------------------------------------------------------------
THE CORE MISTAKE
------------------------------------------------------------

People thought:
  "Black has come up 25 times. Red MUST appear now to balance things out."

The reality:

  Probability of 26 consecutive blacks (from the start):
    (1/2)^26 = 1 / 67,108,864   ← incredibly unlikely

  Probability of black on the 26th spin, given the previous 25 were black:
    1/2   ← exactly the same as always

The roulette wheel has no memory.
Each spin is completely independent of every spin before it.
The wheel does not know it landed on black 25 times.
It does not "owe" anyone a red.

------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THE FALLACY FEELS TRUE
------------------------------------------------------------

The Law of Large Numbers says that over millions of spins,
red and black will appear roughly equally often.

People misread this to mean:
  "So if black is ahead right now, red must catch up soon."

But that's not how it works. The law operates through
DILUTION, not CORRECTION.

  After 25 blacks in a row (25 blacks, 0 reds):
  Ratio = 100% black

  After 1,000 more spins (~500 red, ~500 black):
  Total: ~525 black, ~500 red
  Ratio ≈ 51.2% black — already converging toward 50%

  After 100,000 more spins:
  Ratio ≈ 50.02% black

The early streak gets mathematically drowned out by the
volume of future spins. It is never "paid back."

------------------------------------------------------------
INDEPENDENT EVENTS — THE KEY IDEA
------------------------------------------------------------

An event is independent if its outcome is not affected
by any previous outcome.

A fair coin flip is independent.
A fair roulette spin is independent.
A lottery draw is independent.

For independent events:
  P(heads | last 10 flips were heads) = P(heads) = 0.5

The conditional probability is identical to the unconditional
probability. History is irrelevant.

------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE PEOPLE ACTUALLY GO WRONG
------------------------------------------------------------

The fallacy is everywhere:

  "I've lost 5 times in a row, I'm due for a win."
  "This slot machine hasn't paid out in hours, it must be ready."
  "The lottery numbers 1,2,3,4,5,6 never come up."
    → They are exactly as likely as any other combination.

All of these assume the system is trying to balance itself.
It isn't. Random is random.

------------------------------------------------------------
THE ONE PLACE DEPENDENCE IS REAL
------------------------------------------------------------

If you draw cards from a deck WITHOUT replacement,
then the events ARE dependent.

  P(second card is an ace | first card was an ace) = 3/51
  P(second card is an ace | first card was not an ace) = 4/51

This is different because the sample space changed.
The deck "remembers" — but only because a card was removed.

A roulette wheel resets every spin. Nothing is removed.
This is why casino games are designed the way they are.

------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY
------------------------------------------------------------

  Law of Large Numbers:   The fraction converges over time.
  Gambler's Fallacy:      Assuming the short run must correct itself.
  Reality:                Each spin is independent. History means nothing.

  The casino always wins — not because of luck,
  but because the math is on their side every single spin.
