============================================================
  GAMBLER'S FALLACY
============================================================

THE FAMOUS INCIDENT:

  "On August 18, 1913, at the casino in Monte Carlo, black came up
  a record twenty-six times in succession [in roulette]... There was
  a near-panicky rush to bet on red, beginning around the time black
  had come up a phenomenal fifteen times in a row."
                                    — Huff and Geis, How to Take a Chance

Every person betting on red in that room was reasoning from
the same flawed premise: that the universe was accumulating
a debt to red, and that debt would soon be repaid.
It would not be.

------------------------------------------------------------
THE CORE MISTAKE
------------------------------------------------------------

The reasoning went:
  "Black has appeared 25 times consecutively.
   Red must come now to restore balance."

The mathematical reality is as follows:

  Probability of 26 consecutive blacks from the start:
    (1/2)^26 = 1 / 67,108,864   ← extraordinarily unlikely

  Probability of black on the 26th spin,
  given the previous 25 were black:
    1/2   ← identical to any other spin

The roulette wheel has no memory. Each spin is a completely
independent event. The wheel does not register that it landed
on black 25 times, and it carries no obligation to produce red.

------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THE FALLACY FEELS COMPELLING
------------------------------------------------------------

The Law of Large Numbers states that over millions of spins,
red and black will appear roughly equally often.

The intuitive but incorrect reading of this is:
  "If black is currently ahead, red must catch up soon."

The law does not operate through correction — it operates
through dilution. The early streak is not compensated for;
it is overwhelmed by the sheer volume of future outcomes.

  After 25 consecutive blacks (25 black, 0 red):
  Current ratio: 100% black

  After 1,000 more spins (~500 red, ~500 black):
  Running total: ~525 black, ~500 red
  Ratio: ≈ 51.2% black

  After 100,000 more spins:
  Ratio: ≈ 50.02% black

The 25-spin anomaly is statistically erased not by reversal,
but by being rendered increasingly insignificant relative to
the total sample size.

------------------------------------------------------------
INDEPENDENT EVENTS
------------------------------------------------------------

An event is statistically independent if its outcome is not
influenced by any prior outcome.

  A fair coin flip is independent.
  A fair roulette spin is independent.
  A lottery draw is independent.

For any independent event:
  P(heads | previous 10 flips were heads) = P(heads) = 0.5

The conditional probability is identical to the unconditional
probability. Prior outcomes carry zero predictive power.

------------------------------------------------------------
MANIFESTATIONS OF THE FALLACY
------------------------------------------------------------

The Gambler's Fallacy appears across many domains:

  "Five consecutive losses — a win is overdue."
  "This slot machine hasn't paid out in hours, it must be due."
  "The lottery sequence 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 never gets drawn."
    → It is exactly as probable as any other specific combination.

In each case, the underlying assumption is that the system
is self-correcting. It is not. Each trial is a fresh draw
with no connection to what came before.

------------------------------------------------------------
WHERE DEPENDENCE IS GENUINELY PRESENT
------------------------------------------------------------

It is worth noting the one common scenario where prior
outcomes do affect future probabilities: sampling without
replacement.

Drawing cards from a standard deck:
  P(2nd card is an ace | 1st card was an ace) = 3/51
  P(2nd card is an ace | 1st card was not an ace) = 4/51

Here, conditional probability differs from unconditional
probability because the sample space itself changes after
each draw. One card is permanently removed.

A roulette wheel resets completely after every spin.
Nothing is removed. This is precisely why casino games
using spinning wheels are designed to be independent —
independence is what makes the house edge permanent and
unbeatable through any sequencing strategy.

------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY
------------------------------------------------------------

  Law of Large Numbers:  The fraction converges over many trials.
  Gambler's Fallacy:     The false belief that the short run
                         must self-correct.
  Statistical Reality:   Each spin is independent.
                         History carries no predictive weight.

  The casino does not win through luck.
  It wins because the expected return is negative on every
  single spin — and no sequencing of bets changes that.
