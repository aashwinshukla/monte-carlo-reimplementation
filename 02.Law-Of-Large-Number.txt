============================================================
  LAW OF LARGE NUMBERS
============================================================

FORMAL DEFINITION:
  In repeated independent tests with the same actual probability p
  of a particular outcome in each test, the chance that the fraction
  of times that outcome occurs differs from p converges to zero
  as the number of trials goes to infinity.

  In plain English:
    The more times you repeat an experiment, the closer your
    observed frequency gets to the true underlying probability.

------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IT MEANS IN PRACTICE
------------------------------------------------------------

Flip a fair coin (p = 0.5 for heads).

  After 10 flips  → you might see 70% heads. That's fine.
  After 100 flips → you'll likely see something like 55-45.
  After 10,000 flips → you'll be very close to 50-50.
  After 1,000,000 flips → almost exactly 50-50.

The LAW doesn't say short runs will be balanced.
It says long runs WILL converge.

------------------------------------------------------------
TWO VERSIONS OF THE LAW
------------------------------------------------------------

Weak Law of Large Numbers:
  For any small epsilon > 0, as n → ∞, the probability that the
  sample mean differs from the true mean by more than epsilon
  approaches 0. (Convergence in probability)

Strong Law of Large Numbers:
  The sample mean will almost surely converge to the true mean
  as n → ∞. (Almost sure convergence — a stronger statement)

  For this project, the weak law is all you need to think about.

------------------------------------------------------------
WHY THIS IS THE FOUNDATION OF MONTE CARLO
------------------------------------------------------------

Monte Carlo works BECAUSE of the Law of Large Numbers.

When we estimate π by throwing random darts:
  True probability of landing inside the circle = π/4
  Observed fraction after enough darts → converges to π/4
  So: 4 × (observed fraction) → converges to π

The law guarantees that the simulation will eventually give
the correct answer — if you run it long enough.

------------------------------------------------------------
RATE OF CONVERGENCE
------------------------------------------------------------

The Law tells us convergence happens, but not how fast.
That's given by the error formula:

  Error ≈ 1 / sqrt(N)

  N = 100       → error ≈ 10%
  N = 10,000    → error ≈ 1%
  N = 1,000,000 → error ≈ 0.1%

To halve the error, you need to quadruple the sample size.
This is slow — but it works in ANY number of dimensions.

------------------------------------------------------------
GAMBLER'S FALLACY — WHAT THE LAW DOES NOT SAY
------------------------------------------------------------

Common wrong belief:
  "I've flipped 10 heads in a row. The next flip is more
   likely to be tails to even things out."

This is the GAMBLER'S FALLACY — and it is completely wrong.

The Law of Large Numbers does NOT say that outcomes must
balance out in the short run. Each flip is independent.
The coin has no memory. The probability of tails on the
next flip is still exactly 0.5.

What actually happens over the long run:
  The 10-head streak gets "diluted" by the thousands of
  future flips — it doesn't get "corrected."

  Example:
    After 10 flips  → 10 heads, 0 tails → 100% heads
    After 1000 flips → say 510 heads, 490 tails → 51% heads
    After 100000 flips → ~50003 heads → ~50.003% heads

  The absolute difference (heads - tails) may actually GROW,
  but the FRACTION converges to 0.5. That is the law.

  (This will be explored further in the Gambler's Fallacy file.)
